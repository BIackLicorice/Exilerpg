Gear & forge

Gear

How gear works in Exile RPG - equipment slots, item levels, who loot is rolled for, and how often monsters drop it.

Gear at a glance

Exile RPG gear is more than vanilla swords and armour. Every piece has a rarity, an item level, base numbers (damage, attack speed, armour) that grow with that level, and stat affixes that add bonus levels to your stats.

You get gear in three main ways:

  • Monster drops, rolled for you when something dies.
  • Rewards: dungeon chests, bounties and quests.
  • The forge: you make it yourself with Blacksmithing.

Equipment slots

A piece only counts in its own slot. A helmet held in your hand gives you nothing.

Slot What goes there
Main hand Weapons: swords, axes, maces, daggers, bows, staves, Mage Blades, standards
Off hand Shields and a few special off-hand items
Head, chest, legs, feet Armour
Amulet slots (3) Amulets: the three slots in the right-hand column of your main inventory

Your gear is checked a few times a second. Your stats update whenever you change what you wear or hold.

Reading a tooltip

From top to bottom, a gear tooltip shows:

  1. [Rarity] Name, coloured by rarity.
  2. Base name (on Rares and up), type and slot, e.g. Stiletto · Dagger · Main Hand.
  3. Level N · usable from character level N.
  4. Made for (armour only): the class the piece was rolled for.
  5. A bow's Draw Power (+% arrow damage).
  6. The item's skill, if it has one: a signature ability, an active or passive skill, a set bonus or set ultimate.
  7. Its affixes in their sockets (◆ filled, ◇ open), how many times it was rerolled, its flavour text and lore, and the marks of any orbs used on it.

Item level and the level lock

Every piece has an item level. A piece works only once your character level is at least its item level. Below that level:

  • Weapons deal no damage. Bows' arrows do nothing. Nothing casts from the weapon: no class abilities, skill gems, signature skills or item actives. No mana or cooldown is spent.
  • Armour, shields and amulets give no armour and no stats. They also don't count toward set bonuses.

The first time you equip a locked piece, a chat line tells you which level it needs. While you hold a locked weapon, your ability bar shows the warning. Gear never expires: once a piece works for you, it keeps working at every level after.

Higher item levels mean bigger base numbers. A sword's damage, for example, comes from its item level plus its rarity, so a fresh drop at your level usually beats an old piece. See weapons and armour for the numbers.

Who loot is for

Gear is rolled for one player at the moment it drops, and then it flies to that player:

  • The killer gets the first piece. Any further pieces go round the other players nearby in turn (within 24 blocks, or 48 for bosses).
  • The piece's item level is the receiver's level, or up to 3 below it. A level 20 player gets level 17, 18, 19 or 20 drops.
  • Armour is made for the receiver's class 70% of the time and for another class 30% of the time, so you will sometimes find a piece for a friend.
  • The floating label over a dropped piece shows its rarity, its name, its level and who it was rolled for. Anyone can pick it up and trade it.

When you're alone, loot sprays out in a ring around the body. In a group, each piece flies to the player it was made for. Each dropped item also shows a coloured beam for its rarity.

How often monsters drop gear

Monsters come in ranks: Normal, Magic, Rare and Champion. Mini-bosses and bosses rank above those.

Monster Gear drop Orb drop
Normal 1.5% 1%
Magic 7.5% 5%
Rare 50% 20%
Champion always always
Mini-boss / boss always 1 piece, plus a 50% chance of one more for each extra player nearby always

Each gear drop then rolls its rarity from a table based on the monster's level. Tougher monsters roll as if they were higher level. That table, and the chance that a drop rolls nothing at all, are on the rarity page.

Some drops are not rolled gear but uniques or set pieces: a few older sets and named items such as Emberbrand, Ghostfang and Owleye Cowl. When a drop rolls Common, Uncommon or Rare, the chance of a named item instead is:

Monster Chance of a unique or set piece
Normal 5%
Magic 10%
Rare 20%
Champion and up 50%

What kind of piece drops

If you have a class, about 45% of rolled drops are weapons, 10% are shields and 45% are armour. Of the weapons, 70% are your own class's type and 30% can be any class's. If you have no class yet (classes open at level 5), every kind is equally likely. Weapon bases also have a level band: the stronger bases only start dropping once you reach their band (see weapons).

Very rare drops

On top of the normal drop, Rare monsters and Champions have a tiny chance of a Legendary weapon, and Champions an even smaller chance of an Extraordinary or God's Tool weapon. Monsters also drop forge ingredients and modifiers.

Other ways to get gear

  • Dungeon reward chests roll the rarity table as if the run were 10 levels higher, and they never come up empty.
  • Bounty and quest gear rolls the same table at your level, read further down for the better rewards (like a Rare monster or a Champion), and is never empty.
  • The forge makes exactly the piece you choose, at any level up to your Blacksmithing level.

Selling

Brenna Ironhand and The Collector buy gear, ingredients, modifiers and orbs at one shared price table. Brenna's menu has [Sell what you hold] and [Sell all gear]. See coins and bank.
